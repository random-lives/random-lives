# Egypt's contribution to named individuals in history

*Created 2026-06-21 13:21.*

## Answer (TL;DR)

Bronze Age / pharaonic Egypt (to ~1000 BCE) contributes a **modest, heavily elite- and monument-biased** named record — on the order of **~50,000–100,000+ distinct individuals**, dominated by tomb-owners, officials, and royalty who could afford stone, with the bulk supplied by the Middle and early New Kingdom record now catalogued in the **PNM** prosopography (which counts ~13,000 persons for the 18th Dynasty before Akhenaten alone). The one dense non-elite pharaonic corpus, the New Kingdom workmen's village of **Deir el-Medina**, names on the order of **~1,000–2,000 distinct individuals** across its ~200-year documented life (the famous "~120 workmen" is just the active gang at any one time). The big Egyptian number — Trismegistos People's **~389,000 distinct individuals** — is **overwhelmingly Greco-Roman**: TM coverage only starts at 800 BCE, and its bulk is Greek documentary papyri peaking in the 1st–3rd centuries CE, so the pre-330 BCE (Late Period) share is small, plausibly **well under 10%**, and the true Bronze Age share is near zero. On datasets: there is **no single clean sampling frame** for pharaonic Egypt — a pharaonic name-count means summing separate monument/archive corpora, not querying one database. The richest text corpus, the **Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae** (~1.7M lemma tokens, ~11,000 texts, 3000 BCE–300 CE), is search-only on its own site but is effectively downloadable as the derived, CC-BY-SA **AES / AED-TEI** corpus on GitHub. The **Deir el-Medina Database** (~3,000 text entries) is freely search-only, with no published person count and no bulk download.

## 1. Deir el-Medina — the one dense non-elite pharaonic corpus

The Ramesside (19th–20th Dynasty, c. 1292–1077 BCE) royal-tomb-builders' village is the single richest window onto ordinary named Egyptians, thanks to thousands of surviving ostraca.

| Metric | Figure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Peak population | ~1,200 people | Standard estimate for the Ramesside village ([World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Deir_el-Medina/)). |
| Active gang (workmen) | ~120 (often cited ~113) | The crew at any one time — *not* the cumulative named total. |
| Davies prosopography | ~95 family/individual studies, 47 genealogical charts | [Davies (1999)](https://archive.org/stream/whoswhoatdeirelmedina_201906/Whos%20Who%20at%20Deir%20el%20Medina_djvu.txt), *Who's Who at Deir el-Medina*; 2nd ed. [Davies (2025)](https://www.meretsegerbooks.com/pages/books/M14027/) reports 534 pp., 62 charts. Covers gang families across multiple generations — hundreds of named persons, but no explicit aggregate total is stated. |
| Supply/service personnel | ~600 men, from ~1,500 sources | A separate study of the village's *external* suppliers (porters, water-carriers, woodcutters, etc.) — distinct from the gang itself. |

**Distinct-individual estimate:** combining the gang families (Davies), their wives and children (the genealogies span 3+ generations), scribes/officials, and the ~600 supply personnel, the village over its documented life named on the order of **~1,000–2,000 distinct individuals** — far more than the ~120 active workmen, but still a "village's worth," not a population. (No single source publishes a clean aggregate; this is the order of magnitude implied by the prosopographies.)

**Deir el-Medina Database** (Leiden, *A Survey of the New Kingdom Non-literary Texts from Deir el-Medina*; dmd.wepwawet.nl): a search tool over the corpus of non-literary ostraca/papyri, **~3,000 text entries** ([IAE](https://www.iae-egyptology.org/archives/723)). **Freely searchable online, no bulk download, and it does not publish a distinct-person count** — you would have to harvest persons from individual records.

## 2. Old Kingdom (c. 2700–2180 BCE)

Named individuals survive almost entirely as **tomb-owners and titled officials** on stone — the classic monument bias.

- **Strudwick (1985)**, *The Administration of Egypt in the Old Kingdom*, prosopographizes the holders of the highest administrative titles (viziers, overseers) — a catalogue on the order of a few hundred top officials, the elite of the elite.
- **Digital Giza** (Harvard) organizes the Giza necropolis around individual mastaba tombs and their owners; the project digitized metadata for ~20,000 finds and thousands of tombs ([Digital Giza](http://giza.fas.harvard.edu/)), but tombs (hence named owners) number in the low thousands at this one site.
- **Porter & Moss**, *Topographical Bibliography*, is the standard monument index — the de facto frame for "where named Old Kingdom people are attested," but it is a bibliography, not a person-count.

**Order of magnitude:** named Old Kingdom individuals run to the **low thousands** — overwhelmingly elite, concentrated in the Memphite cemeteries (Giza, Saqqara).

## 3. Middle Kingdom and early New Kingdom (c. 2050–1350 BCE)

- **PNM — "Persons and Names of the Middle Kingdom and Early New Kingdom"** (Ilin-Tomich, Univ. Mainz) is the authoritative aggregator and the single biggest driver of Egypt's Bronze Age count. It catalogues **distinct persons** (not just name-types) from Mentuhotep II to Amenhotep III, and is "envisaged to cover about **35,000 attestations** when finished"; **Version 5 alone added ~13,000 persons for the 18th Dynasty before Akhenaten** ([Ilin-Tomich (2024), Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/13334187); [PNM, Mainz](https://pnm.uni-mainz.de/info)). This implies a Middle/early-New-Kingdom distinct-individual count in the **high thousands to low tens of thousands** — far above the "low thousands" a monuments-only view suggests, and the reason Egypt's pharaonic total lands at ~50,000–100,000+ rather than ~10,000.
- **Kahun / Lahun papyri** (the pyramid-town of Senusret II), published by [Griffith (1898)](https://archive.org/details/hieraticpapyrifr00grifuoft): household "census" lists recording all members of individual households across up to three generations plus servants, plus lists of work-gangs, priests, soldiers, and scribes — the rare Middle Kingdom non-elite naming source. Hundreds of named townsfolk, now subsumed into PNM.
- **Order of magnitude: high thousands to ~10,000+ distinct named individuals** for the Middle Kingdom proper, with PNM as the authoritative count.

## 4. Trismegistos People — the pharaonic-vs-Greco-Roman split

This is where Egypt's "big number" comes from, and it is **not Bronze Age**.

| Database | Count | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Person records (distinct individuals) | **375,091** (≈389k in later releases) | [AWOL/TM People](http://ancientworldonline.blogspot.com/2022/03/trismegistos-people.html) |
| Name attestations (references) | **535,569** (568,833 in a later count) | TM REF database |
| Distinct names | **36,413** | TM NAM database |
| Egyptian-language attestations | **196,409** | But coverage of Demotic/abnormal hieratic is "lacunose" |

**Coverage:** non-royal individuals in **documentary** texts in Egypt, **800 BCE – 800 CE** ([TM People About](https://www.trismegistos.org/ref/about.php)). Pharaohs, emperors, and saints are excluded.

**The split:**
- TM **does not begin until 800 BCE**, so the entire Bronze Age (Old/Middle/New Kingdom) is **outside its frame** — Egypt's pharaonic named record is not in this number at all.
- The corpus is built on **Greek documentary papyri** (the founding distillation was ~375,000 references from ~50,000 Greek papyri/ostraca; [Depauw & Van Beek (2009)](https://www.trismegistos.org/ref/depauw_vanbeek.pdf)). Greek papyri **peak in the 2nd century CE (~11,000 texts)**, with the mass of attestations Ptolemaic-through-Byzantine.
- The pre-330 BCE Late Period (Saite/Persian, 800–330 BCE) is represented only by the thinner, explicitly "lacunose" Demotic/abnormal-hieratic material.

**Bottom line:** of TM's ~389k individuals, the Greco-Roman era (after ~330 BCE) is **the overwhelming majority** — plausibly **>90%** — and the pre-330 BCE Late Period a small remainder. *(I could not find a published exact century-by-century person breakdown; this split is inferred from coverage start-date, the Greek-papyri foundation, and the explicit thinness of pre-Ptolemaic Egyptian material. A precise figure would require querying TM's date metadata directly, which is partly paywalled.)*

## 5. Ranke, *Die ägyptischen Personennamen* — onomasticon size (names, not individuals)

Hermann **Ranke**'s 3-volume reference ([1935](http://giza.fas.harvard.edu/pubdocs/309/full/), 1952, 1977) is the standard catalogue of distinct ancient Egyptian **personal names** (name-types). Vol. I (*Verzeichnis der Namen*) is 432 pp. of name entries. The commonly cited scale of the whole-of-Egyptian-history onomasticon is on the order of **~10,000+ distinct name-types** — *(I could not confirm Ranke's exact total; flag as approximate. This is a count of distinct names, not of individuals or attestations.)*

For the Old Kingdom specifically, [Scheele-Schweitzer (2014)](https://www.harrassowitz-verlag.de/title_1454.ahtml), *Die Personennamen des Alten Reiches*, verifies **~3,800 distinct personal names** (name-types) — a clean, modern figure for one period's onomasticon. Again: name-types, not individuals.

## Datasets of writing — availability summary

| Dataset | Scope | Size | Coverage | Downloadable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae** (BBAW Berlin) | Lemmatized hieroglyphic/hieratic/Demotic texts | ~1.69M lemma tokens; ~11,000 texts | **3000 BCE – 300 CE** (pharaonic + Greco-Roman) | **Search-only on tla.digital** (raw-data release "in preparation"); but the derived **AES / AED-TEI** corpus is on GitHub under **CC-BY-SA 4.0** ([github.com/simondschweitzer/aes](https://github.com/simondschweitzer/aes), 100,000+ sentences) — effectively the downloadable form. |
| **PNM** (Persons and Names of the MK & early NK, Mainz) | Distinct persons, names, titles, sources | ~13,000+ persons (18th Dyn. pre-Akhenaten alone); ~35,000 attestations envisaged | Middle Kingdom → Amenhotep III | Distinct-**person** database (Linked Open Data); dataset on **Zenodo** ([Ilin-Tomich 2024](https://zenodo.org/records/13334187)). The one real pharaonic person-index. |
| **Deir el-Medina Database** (Leiden) | Non-literary Ramesside ostraca/papyri | ~3,000 text entries | New Kingdom Ramesside | **Search-only**, free; no bulk download; no person count published. |
| **Trismegistos** (KU Leuven) | Documentary texts + people, Egypt | ~389k persons; ~536k attestations; ~36k names | **800 BCE – 800 CE** (skews Greco-Roman) | Search-only; many functions **paywalled** for non-subscribers; data export limited. |

**Sampling-frame verdict:** **No uniform sampling frame exists for pharaonic Egypt.** TLA is a *text* corpus (lemmatized words, downloadable via AES), not a *person* index, and it does not enumerate distinct individuals. TM is a person index but starts at 800 BCE, so it excludes the entire Bronze Age. The one true pharaonic person-index, **PNM** (downloadable on Zenodo), covers the Middle and early New Kingdom but not the Old Kingdom or the later New Kingdom. So even with PNM, a full pharaonic named-individual count is necessarily **"sum the separate monument and archive corpora"** (Middle/early-New-Kingdom persons via PNM; Giza/Saqqara tomb-owners via Digital Giza + Porter & Moss; Old Kingdom officials via Strudwick; Kahun households via Griffith; Deir el-Medina via Davies and the Leiden DMD) — not "sample one dataset."
